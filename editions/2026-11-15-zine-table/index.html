Which booklet pays for the table?

You can collect $24 in sales and still come up short on a $24 table fee. Work out what part of each sale is free to pay the fee.

Sort one sale into two envelopes

A zine is a small self-published booklet. Suppose you rent a table for $24 at a one-day fair. You make each booklet after someone buys one. Each $6 sale uses $2 of paper and ink. Put that $2 back in the materials envelope. The $4 left can go toward the table.

At four sales, the till has taken $24. You spent $8 making those copies and still owe the $24 table fee. The day is $8 short. Six sales bring in $36. Subtract $12 for copies and $24 for the table and the two sides balance exactly. That is the break-even point for this example.

Run the book table

Keep the price at $6 and the table at $24. First predict what changes when paper and ink cost $3 per copy. Then try a cost equal to the selling price.

Choose your example Receipts $36. Materials $12. Table $24. Exactly even. First whole-copy break-even: 6.

Write a budget another person can check

Fold a sheet of paper into columns for copies, receipts, materials, table and balance. Try five, six and seven copies at the original $2 material cost. The balances are $4 short, exactly even, and $4 above the costs included here. Give a friend just the costs and have them predict where the sign changes.

For a second round make the table cost $25. Each $4 contribution pays down that fee. Six copies leave $1 owing. Seven leave $3 over. Whenever a positive fee divided by a positive contribution produces a fraction, round up to find the first whole copy that covers the fee.

Where the example ends. This is a made-to-order, one-product exercise. It counts only the table and materials. Upfront printing, unsold stock, your time or payment fees require a different budget. The exercise estimates cost coverage and makes no sales forecast.

Check your reasoning

Why do four $6 sales leave the table short?

Materials took $8 of the $24 collected. Only $16 is available toward the $24 table.

When do you cover the fee if each copy costs $3 to make?

Eight copies. Each sale contributes $3, and eight contributions cover $24.

What if every dollar of the $6 goes to materials?

Each sale contributes zero to the positive table fee. There is no finite sales count that covers it on these terms.
