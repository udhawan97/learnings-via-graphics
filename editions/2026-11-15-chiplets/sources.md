# Sources and claim boundaries

Checked November 15, 2026. Product examples remain attributed to their publishers; the Nova example and lesson diagram are original teaching devices.

## Primary sources

1. **UCIe Consortium, “How UCIe will Enable Broad Chiplet Adoption,” March 31, 2023.**

   https://www.uciexpress.org/post/how-ucie-will-enable-broad-chiplet-adoption

   Supports the explanations of exceeding a single die’s reticle area, choosing a process for each functional chiplet, mixing chiplet counts and types, and using package-level links.

2. **UCIe Consortium, “Specifications,” undated; accessed November 15, 2026.**

   https://www.uciexpress.org/specifications

   Supports the description of UCIe as an open package-level die-to-die standard covering physical, protocol and software layers, including compliance testing. The lesson does not claim that the standard alone guarantees a working product.

3. **AMD, “AMD Chiplet Ecosystem,” white paper, December 9, 2024.**

   https://www.amd.com/content/dam/amd/en/documents/solutions/technologies/chiplet-architecture-white-paper.pdf

   Supports the stated opportunities—smaller dies, process choice, modular reuse and parallel development—and the cautions about communication power and latency, packaging cost, system yield and design complexity.

4. **Intel Foundry, “Advanced Packaging Innovations,” undated; accessed November 15, 2026.**

   https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/foundry/packaging.html

   Supports the explanation that multi-die systems depend on package interconnect and advanced test work. Vendor-specific performance claims are not used.

## Claim boundaries

- “Nova” is invented; its core counts illustrate reuse, not a commercial processor.
- The diagram is conceptual, not to scale and not a physical floorplan.
- Manufacturing yield, cost, power and performance depend on design and production evidence. No universal chiplet advantage is claimed.
- UCIe standardizes connection layers. Functional compatibility, security, firmware, power delivery, thermal design, packaging and qualification remain system-level work.
