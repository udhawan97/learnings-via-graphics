Where do the other two units of heat come from?

The meter records one unit of electricity. The room receives three units of heat. Trace both inputs to find the missing energy.

Follow the fluid around the loop

A refrigerant is a fluid that carries heat as it circulates. The outdoor coil lets a cold refrigerant absorb heat from outdoor air. A compressor powered by electricity raises the refrigerant’s pressure and temperature. At the indoor coil the warmer refrigerant releases heat. Its pressure then falls through an expansion device, and it can collect heat again.

The warmth you feel near a refrigerator’s back coils is a familiar example of the same cycle. Home air-source heat pumps already use this cycle. The question for a particular installation is how much heat it delivers under the conditions being measured.

Balance the energy

Give the model exactly 1 kWh of electricity. Predict what reaches the room if the outdoor coil collects a different amount of heat during the same hour.

Choose your example 2 kWh outdoors + 1 kWh electricity = 3 kWh indoors. Heating COP = 3.

Check an energy claim on paper

Make three columns headed “electricity”, “heat picked up” and “heat delivered”. With electricity fixed at 1 kWh, try outside contributions of 1, 2 and 3 kWh. The delivered totals are 2, 3 and 4 kWh. Hide one cell and let a partner recover it from the other two.

Heating coefficient of performance, or COP, tells you how much useful heat is delivered per unit of electricity consumed. Use the same time period and units for both. If 2 kWh of electricity delivers 6 kWh of heat in another example, its COP is still 3.

Where the example ends. This steady-state model assumes the two inputs reach the room as heat. A measured rating depends on temperatures, test conditions and which equipment is included. A season also includes changing loads, defrosting and sometimes backup heating.

Check your reasoning

Where did the extra 2 kWh enter the system?

Through the outdoor coil, as heat taken from outdoor air.

How do you get a heating COP of 3?

Divide 3 kWh of delivered heat by 1 kWh of electricity, measured over the same period.

Does one hour tell you the winter bill?

You would also need the building’s heating demand, weather, system operation and electricity price over the season.
