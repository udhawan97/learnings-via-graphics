Which path climbs more steeply?

Two pencil lines climb forty metres. Put a finger on each path and see where the map makes you do the most climbing in the least distance.

A contour is a line you could walk level

Imagine marking the edge of a pond at one water level. Every mark sits at the same height. A contour draws that kind of line on a map, even where there is no water. Move across the curves and the labels tell you whether you are going up or down.

Read the map key for the vertical interval. Then follow the horizontal scale bar for distance along your path as seen from above. In our sketch, the sequence is 100, 110, 120, 130, 140 metres. Five lines enclose four gaps. Both paths climb 40 metres without dipping along the way.

Stretch the climb

Keep the rise at 40 metres. Guess the grade when the horizontal distance doubles, then change the distance to check.

Choose your example 40 m ÷ 100 m = 40% average grade.

Make a pocket map key

On paper draw five contour marks, ten metres apart in height. Sketch three paths rising across all five, using 100, 200 and 400 metres of horizontal travel. Write down the rise, the run and your predicted grade for each. Fold over the answers and ask someone to rank the climbs from the sketch alone.

Your answer key is 40%, 20% and 10%. To make a counterexample, add a dip to a route. The final height minus the starting height now misses some climbing. Read each uphill stretch separately when estimating total ascent.

Where the example ends. Grade is rise divided by horizontal distance. A 40% grade corresponds to 40 metres up per 100 metres across. Surface, weather and trail access need separate evidence before using a real route.

Check your reasoning

Why are there four height changes across five contours?

The changes happen between neighbouring lines. Each of the four gaps adds 10 m.

Which distance gives a 20% average grade?

200 horizontal metres. Divide 40 by 200, then multiply by 100.

Would reversing the walk change its steepness?

The direction changes to downhill. On the same path, the magnitude of the average grade stays the same.
