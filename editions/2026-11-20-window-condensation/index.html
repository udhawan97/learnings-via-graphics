Why does the inside of a window fog up?

Indoor window fog appears when the room-side glass is colder than the air's dew point. The wet glass is a clue, not a diagnosis: dry it, record where it returns, reduce obvious moisture sources, and investigate persistent dampness.

EVERYDAY LIFE / DAD'S FIELD NOTES · HOMEFULL GUIDE: 9 MIN · PRACTICE: 20 MIN

01 · Visual model

One threshold decides whether vapor can stay vapor

Text equivalent: The room air contains water vapor. Its dew point is 12°C. A 15°C pane is warmer than that threshold, so the simple model keeps the water as vapor. An 8°C pane is colder, so some vapor becomes droplets on the glass. The model compares temperatures; it does not diagnose why the pane is cold or why the air is moist.

02 · Read the clue

First locate the water

Room side

Warm indoor air met a colder interior surface. Indoor moisture, surface temperature, air movement and outdoor weather can all contribute.

Outdoor side

Outdoor air met an exterior surface below its dew point. That is a different air mass and does not by itself prove excess indoor humidity.

Apparently between panes

The moisture is not reachable from either exposed face. Record it and use the window maker's guidance or a qualified professional rather than dismantling the unit.

03 · Foundations

Dew point is not the same as relative humidity

Dew point is the temperature at which air with the same amount of water vapor reaches saturation. Relative humidity compares current moisture with the amount the air could hold at its current temperature. Cooling air next to a pane can raise its local relative humidity to 100% even while the room's displayed relative humidity stays lower.

A window can cross the threshold because the air carries more moisture, the pane becomes colder, or both. Better-insulating windows generally keep their interior surface warmer; DOE describes condensation resistance as a product's ability to resist interior moisture or frost in cold weather.

The readings are invented to expose the rule. A real pane has changing temperatures, airflow and materials; an inexpensive room meter may estimate humidity but does not prove the temperature of every surface.

Field wisdom: Treat one wet window as an observation to map. Dry it, note the side and location, and see whether the same pattern returns under similar conditions.

05 · Reality check

What the droplets cannot tell you

  • Condensation does not identify a single cause. Indoor humidity, a cold bridge, closed coverings, restricted airflow, a leak or several factors may overlap.
  • A humidity target is not universal. Climate, construction, occupants and equipment matter; follow building and equipment guidance.
  • Do not paint or caulk over recurring dampness, dismantle a sealed pane, or disturb visible mold to complete this exercise.
  • Dry wet surfaces promptly. Renters should report persistent moisture; widespread dampness, hidden leaks or visible mold may need qualified help.

06 · Practice

Make a 20-minute condensation map

  1. Without taking the window apart, mark whether moisture is inside, outside or apparently between panes.
  2. Sketch where it is heaviest: center, edge, frame or sill. Note weather, cooking, showering, laundry and closed coverings.
  3. Wipe the exposed wet surface dry. Record room relative humidity if you already have a meter.
  4. Choose one reversible, safe action supported by the space and equipment: use an outdoor-vented bath or kitchen exhaust, move a covering enough to restore airflow, or follow a dehumidifier's instructions.
  5. Define success before the next similar day: less area wet, fewer droplets, or no water on the sill. If it returns or extends beyond the pane, report or investigate it.

Knowledge check

  1. What comparison makes condensation possible?
  2. Why is a room-side droplet a clue rather than a diagnosis?
  3. What makes the observation useful?
Reveal answer guidance
  1. The surface temperature falls below the adjacent air's dew point.
  2. Several moisture, temperature and airflow conditions can create the same visible droplet.
  3. Record the side, location, conditions, one reversible action and a recheck criterion; dry the surface and escalate persistent moisture.
